## 0:05-0:15  1. Why referrals close about 3x better than cold (10 min)

Goal: give them a reason to take the three-name ask seriously, without inventing a Trinity close rate.

### What we can say, and the source

Industry hiring data, not a Summit Trinity number:

Across 1.1 million employee referrals processed through ERIN in 2024, about 13% of referrals became hires, versus 4% from recruiter sourcing (cold) and 2% from job boards. ERIN's own write-up of that table is "Referrals convert 3-6x better than other channels." 13% versus 4% is about 3x better than cold sourcing.

Cite on the slide and in the script, exactly: https://erinapp.com/employee-referrals (ERIN, "Employee Referrals: The Complete Guide," 2024 program data plus the industry benchmarks they print next to it). Fetched for this outline on Nov 13, 2026.

Supporting, not the lead claim: Ashby's talent-trends report on referrals found 40% of referred candidates move from application to interview. URL: https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals. That is interview rate, not hire rate, and Ashby's page as fetched does not print a matching 12% cold figure, so do not hang the "3x" on Ashby.

### How to teach it in this room

- A referred person already has a trusted intro. Cold traffic starts at zero trust.
- Our own files already call this the best source, without a house percentage: "Rep referrals" lead the rookie funnel, and "Referrals from current vets and pillars. These are the best source" leads the vet funnel.
- The ask already lives in three places: the Monday three-names text, rookie interview call 2 ("Who do you know that would kill it out here with you?"), and weekly 1:1 question 10.

### What not to say

- Do not say "our referrals close 3x better" as if it were a Trinity stat. It is not in the recruiting files, the Sales Blueprint extract, or the rookie outline.
- Do not use ERIN's referral-bonus dollar ranges, or any bonus, in this section or anywhere else in the training.
- If Scoots wants a house number on the slide, stop and use his number only after he gives it.

Flag: Trinity's own referral-versus-cold close rate is **UNSOURCED - needs Scoots's number**. The 3x in this section is the ERIN 2024 industry figure above, cited to that URL.

## 0:40-1:00  4. How to run an interview with the new form (20 min)

Goal: every interviewer can fill one form, score Mind, Heart, and Feet 1 to 5, mark yes or no, and write the one reason.

Handout: `/workspace/recruiting/interview-form-2026-11-12.md` (draft for Scoots's review). Call it a seat. They request a seat. When the roster closes, it's closed. Do not write pay numbers. Do not promise a place to stay or a market.

### Flow, before scores

- Space calls a day apart. Book the next one before you hang up.
- Qualify rookie vs vet up front. A vet only counts as a vet once the prior-year number is verified. Counts only, no dollar amounts. If there is no verified number, run them as a rookie or hold the next call until proof is in.
- If they are talking to anyone else at Hawx, stop the pitch. Scoots calls that person first (one-house rule).
- If they are under a contract, flag Counsel and do not pitch.
- Rookie call 1 is getting to know them, after the intro video. Rookie call 2 is pay, schedule, and commitment, after the pay video; they explain pay back on the call, nothing written. Rookie call 3 is the decision, with Colton, at calendly.com/mathewjoyce/rookie-interview-call-3. Vet call 1 is discovery at calendly.com/mathewjoyce/discovery-call; Scoots runs the vet calls.

### Scoring, 1 to 5

**Mind (skill and coachability).** Ask: tell me about yourself (school, work, sports). Say you're stuck at 3 sales a day, what would you do to break past it? How do you take feedback?

- 1: vague, blames the area or other people, gets defensive on feedback.
- 5: a real story, a plan when they're stuck, they want coaching and can name a time they changed after it.

**Heart (desire and why).** Ask: why do you want to do this? What's your best characteristic? What are you most proud of? On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you to start? If they're not a 10, what would get them there?

- 1: "seems cool," or they're only shopping the deal, with no specific why.
- 5: a specific why (school, proving something, or growth), a real win they earned, and they can name the gap to a 10.
- Also log Ready: ______ / 10.

**Feet (activity and commitment).** Ask: tell me about a commitment you kept when you wanted to quit. Anything that could stop you from running a full season, May 1 to August 31? Have you talked to your parents, or do you make your own decisions?

- 1: no commitment story, or a trip or classes they will not move.
- 5: a specific time they wanted to quit and didn't, the calendar is clear, and parents are on board or they decide.

The "3 sales a day" prompt is a coaching question from Scoots's Nov 7 script. It is not a door quota and does not go on a handout as a target.

### Decision

- Yes or no. Then the one reason, written in a sentence.
- Next step if yes, rookie: call 3 with Colton, calendly.com/mathewjoyce/rookie-interview-call-3.
- Next step if yes, vet: discovery call, calendly.com/mathewjoyce/discovery-call, only after the prior-year number is verified when this form is being used mid-process; call 1 vets start on that same link.
- Next step if no: no next-call link.

Partner A asks the Mind, Heart, and Feet questions, scores 1 to 5, marks yes or no, and writes the one reason. Swap roles for 4 minutes. ## 1:00-1:15  5. How to close a recruit (15 min)

Goal: book the next step before you hang up, and know what a yes actually triggers.

Talking points, mapped to the Blueprint close idea without using the door arsenal as a recruit script:
- Control the path: every call ends in a booked next call or a clear no. Do not end on "I'll text you the link later."
- Soft close first, to get the real objection: "What time works tomorrow?" "On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you to start? If you're not a 10, what would get you there?"
- Book it live. Rookie calls a day apart. Call 3 at calendly.com/mathewjoyce/rookie-interview-call-3. Vets book the next Scoots call after proof is in.
- Call 3 close, if they're in (rookie script): "If I offer you the seat, can you make trainings Wednesdays at 7 PM EST?" Go over the 3-week commitment exactly the way the agreement has it. Promise nothing beyond that. No housing or market promise. If they're in, offer the seat, then the agreement goes out only after Scoots OKs it (Nidus). Then set the 1-on-1 and send the training course.
- The takeaway line, from Scoots, if they say they need to think about it: "That's okay, I don't think this is gonna work out, we'll go with someone else." Then see if they fight for it. On a vet, use that line only if Scoots chooses to with that person.
- Offer bar in the rookie script: offer if they're 8 or higher on ready, and both coachability and work ethic are a clear yes. That is a draft scorecard, not a public claim.
- After a verbal yes: agreement through Hawx Nidus, Scoots's OK first. The 2027 rookie agreement still needs Weber review before it is treated as ready to send. Nothing gets signed without that legal gate.
- Log the lead. Add rookies to GETHawx after call 2. Do not invent a signed status.

Vet close, short: proof first, then fit, then a verbal yes, then the EXPERIENCED or VETERAN 2027 agreement after Scoots OKs it. Travel and housing are after signing, and are never promised on the close.

Practice (about 5 minutes): partner says "I need to think about it." Interviewer uses the takeaway line, then waits. Coach the silence. Sales Blueprint: after a close, stop talking and own the silence.

## 1:15-1:30  6. Objections and answers (15 min)

Goal: same formula as the door, used on recruit objections. No pay numbers in the answers.

Formula, every time (from the Blueprint and the rookie outline):
1. Yes. Neutralize it. "Yeah." "Totally." "Of course." Do not debate. Do not flinch.
2. Address. Show you heard them. One relevant next step, not a speech.
3. Close. Ask for the book, the names, or the decision. Then stop talking.

Common recruit objections, drawn from the interview scripts' red flags and closes. Answers below are training prompts for Voice to script in Scoots's voice. They are not approved outbound texts.

| Objection | Direction of the answer (no comp, no housing, no market) |
|---|---|
| "I need to think about it." | Use Scoots's takeaway line, then wait. If they fight for it, book the next call or move to the offer questions. |
| "I have to talk to my parents." | That's expected. Tell them to talk before the next call. Book call 3 anyway. "You might get an offer" is already in the call 2 close. Do not bring parents a pay sheet in writing. |
| "What's the pay?" / shopping the deal | Pay is spoken on the call, scale on screen, never in a text. Get or keep them on the call. Do not quote a number in the room as a sample. |
| Pushback on hours or knocking until dark | Restate the schedule as it is: meeting 9:30 to 10:30 AM, knock noon to dark, Saturdays 10:30 AM to 5:30 PM, Sundays off. Season May 1 to August 31. If they will not run that, it is a Feet no. |
| Summer classes or a trip they will not move | That's a Feet red flag. Do not talk them into moving it. Mark no, or hold, and write the reason. |
| First questions are about time off | Same as hours. Get back to commitment. If that's the whole conversation, it is a no. |
| "I can't think of any names." | Stay on the ask from section 3. Prompt categories (school, sports, work, last summer). Do not accept a stall as the end of the ask. |
| Vague "seems cool" why | Go back to Heart. If they still have no specific why, score it low and do not force a yes. |
| Vet number is vague, or they won't send proof | No proof, no call 2 as a vet. Hold or move them to the rookie track. Scoots decides. |
| Under a contract or talking to someone else at Hawx | Do not pitch. Contract: flag Counsel. One-house: Scoots calls that person first. |
| Want terms in writing before committing (vet) | Deal terms stay spoken. Do not put numbers in a text to win the close. |
| "Can I get housing / which market?" | Do not promise either. Housing and market, if they exist for that person, are whatever the agreement says, after paper. |

Body language and tone carry this, same as the door: if your face argues, the words do not matter.

Practice (about 6 minutes): facilitator throws three objections from the table. The room answers with Yes, Address, Close, then silence. Correct any answer that quotes pay, a bonus, housing, or a market.

## 1:30-1:40  7. First-week playbook for a new rep (10 min)

Goal: a new yes knows what happens in week one after the offer, and the recruiter knows what to set before hanging up.

There is no standalone "first-week playbook" file. This block is built from the 2027 onboarding packet, the rookie interview close, and the 1:1 system. Teach the sequence, not production targets.

Week of the offer (what the recruiter and manager set):
1. Paper. Agreement through Hawx Nidus, only after Scoots OKs the send. The 2027 rookie agreement still needs Weber review, so do not talk as if paper is already cleared to go. Background check when Hawx sends it. Read the 3-week commitment exactly as the agreement states. Do not add housing or market promises on top of it.
2. GETHawx and the team group chat. Check phone and email. "Respond to chat when you can."
3. First 1:1, booked on the offer call, before they start the training course. They bring what they want out of the summer and what would stop them. Record the "sunblock" video (a short video to their future self on why they're doing this). Name a Board of Mentors, 2 to 3 people who keep them in the game.
4. Training course and the pitch, sent after the agreement. Wednesday trainings, 7 PM EST, mandatory. Learn the road map: Intro, Bridge, Price Sheet, Objections, Close. Memorize the skeleton before they riff. Objection formula: Yes, Address, Close, then stop talking.
5. Weekly 1:1 after that, 20 to 30 minutes: last week's commitment, what's in the way, Mind / Heart / Feet, one thing the manager does for them, and question 10 (three names). Book the next 1:1 before you hang up.

Schedule they should already have heard on call 2, so week one in season does not surprise them: meeting 9:30 to 10:30 AM, knock noon to dark, Saturdays 10:30 to 5:30, Sundays off. Season May 1 to August 31. Log sales in GETHawx the same day once they are in market.

From the Blueprint, keep only the input commitments, not the door or account table: pick a wake-up time and do not negotiate it, morning routine, first door picked the night before, stay out, role play even if it is ten minutes. Neutrality: reset every door. No negativity in the car group.

Leave out of this block, on purpose:
- Any dollar, bonus, tier, or weekly-revenue minimum (the 1:1 system and Constitution figures stay off the slide).
- Door-count targets, hours-per-day minimums, and close-rate math from the Blueprint and SalesBlueprint_Summit "Activity is the multiplier" slide.
- Housing addresses, bed rules, airport directions, flight advances, and market assignments. Those are post-sign ops, and nothing in this training promises them.

Goal: nobody leaves without three names written down. This is a capture exercise, not a send.

| Clock | Mins | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1:40-1:44 | 4 | Recap the categories: school, sports, work, teammates, last season, people a vet knows who would want a seat. Repeat the spoken ask. Remind them: names and numbers, not a pitch. |
| 1:44-1:54 | 10 | Silent write. Each rep fills a three-name card: Name, Phone, Rookie or Vet (if they know), How they know them. Aim for three. Two is a miss they can finish before they stand up. |
| 1:54-1:58 | 4 | Pair share. Partner A asks, "Who do you know that would kill it out here with you?" Partner B reads one name and why. Swap. If a card is short, the partner helps pull a category, not a cold stranger. |
| 1:58-2:00 | 2 | Collect the cards. Close: roster closes once seats fill. Headhunter drafts Referrals-tab rows from the cards. Nothing is texted, logged as official, or booked until Scoots OKs the names and any outreach, by person. |
