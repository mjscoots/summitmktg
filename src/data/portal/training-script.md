# 0:05-0:15  1. Why referrals close about 3x better than cold (10 min)

Facilitator notes, not spoken: cite ERIN on the slide if there is one, at https://erinapp.com/employee-referrals. Do not hang the 3x on Ashby. Do not use ERIN bonus dollar ranges. If Scoots wants a house number, stop and use only a number he gives.

Script:

I'm gonna give you a reason to take the three-name ask seriously.

This next part is industry hiring data, not a Trinity number. I do not have our own referral-versus-cold close rate to put on the board today. That number is unsourced. If I get one, I'll tell you. Until then, do not say our referrals close 3 times better.

The figure we can use is from ERIN, 2024. They processed 1.1 million employee referrals. About 13 percent of referrals became hires. Cold recruiter sourcing was 4 percent. Job boards were 2 percent. Their write-up says referrals convert 3 to 6 times better than other channels. 13 versus 4 is about 3 times better than cold sourcing.

The source is erinapp.com/employee-referrals. That's their industry figure, not our close rate.

A referred person already has a trusted intro. Cold traffic starts at zero trust.

Our own files already call referrals the best source, without a house percentage. Rep referrals lead the rookie funnel. Referrals from current vets and pillars lead the vet funnel.

The ask already lives in three places. The Monday three-names text. Rookie interview call 2. Weekly 1:1, question 10. We do all three, every time.

# 0:40-1:00  4. How to run an interview with the new form (20 min)

Facilitator notes, not spoken: handout is `/workspace/recruiting/interview-form-2026-11-12.md`, still a draft. Who fills it on which call is not decided. The "3 sales a day" prompt is a coaching question, not a door quota, and does not go on a handout as a target. Practice about 8 minutes. Follow-up texts are drafts and do not get sent from this training.

Script:

I'm gonna walk the new form. You fill it out. You score Mind, Heart, and Feet, 1 to 5. You mark yes or no. You write the one reason, in a sentence.

Call it a seat. They request a seat. When the roster closes, it's closed. Do not write pay numbers. Do not promise a place to stay or a market.

Before you score, do this.

Space calls a day apart. Book the next one before you hang up.

Qualify rookie or vet up front. A vet only counts as a vet once the prior-year number is verified. Counts only, no dollar amounts. If there is no verified number, run them as a rookie, or hold the next call until proof is in.

If they are talking to anyone else at Hawx, stop the pitch. I call that person first.

If they are under a contract, flag Counsel. Do not pitch.

Rookie call 1 is getting to know them, after the intro video.

Rookie call 2 is pay, schedule, and commitment, after the pay video. They explain pay back on the call. Nothing written.

Rookie call 3 is the decision, with Colton, at calendly.com/mathewjoyce/rookie-interview-call-3.

Vet call 1 is discovery at calendly.com/mathewjoyce/discovery-call. I run the vet calls.

Mind is skill and coachability. Ask: tell me about yourself, school, work, sports. Say you're stuck at 3 sales a day, what would you do to break past it. How do you take feedback.

1 is vague, blames the area or other people, gets defensive on feedback.

5 is a real story, a plan when they're stuck, they want coaching, and they can name a time they changed after it.

That 3 sales a day line is a coaching question. It is not a quota. It does not go on a handout as a target.

Heart is desire and why. Ask: why do you want to do this. What's your best characteristic. What are you most proud of. On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you to start. If they're not a 10, what would get them there.

1 is seems cool, or they're only shopping the deal, with no specific why.

5 is a specific why, school, proving something, or growth, a real win they earned, and they can name the gap to a 10.

Also log ready, out of 10.

Feet is activity and commitment. Ask: tell me about a commitment you kept when you wanted to quit. Anything that could stop you from running a full season, May 1 to August 31. Have you talked to your parents, or do you make your own decisions.

1 is no commitment story, or a trip or classes they will not move.

5 is a specific time they wanted to quit and didn't, the calendar is clear, and parents are on board or they decide.

Then the decision. Yes or no. Then the one reason, written in a sentence.

If yes, and they're a rookie: call 3 with Colton, calendly.com/mathewjoyce/rookie-interview-call-3.

If yes, and they're a vet: discovery call, calendly.com/mathewjoyce/discovery-call, after the prior-year number is verified when this form is mid-process. Call 1 vets start on that same link.

If no: no next-call link.

Follow-up texts are drafts. They need my OK. Do not send them from this training. This is what they look like.

Yes, rookie: Yo, name, good call. Grab the next one with Colton here, calendly.com/mathewjoyce/rookie-interview-call-3

Yes, vet: Yo, name, good call. Grab the next one here, calendly.com/mathewjoyce/discovery-call

No: Hey, gonna pass this time. Door's open if you wanna run it later

Practice, about 8 minutes. Each pair gets a form. Partner B answers as a rookie for 4 minutes. Partner A asks the Mind, Heart, and Feet questions, scores 1 to 5, marks yes or no, and writes the one reason. Then you swap for 4 minutes. I'll spot one form and read the reason out loud. No real names needed if the recruit is made up.

# 1:00-1:15  5. How to close a recruit (15 min)

Facilitator notes, not spoken: do not teach door option, assignment, fear-of-loss, price-drop, or limited-route language as recruit closes. Weber review of the 2027 rookie agreement is still open. Nidus sender is not confirmed in this script. Practice about 5 minutes, then own the silence.

Script:

Every call ends in a booked next call, or a clear no. Do not end on I'll text you the link later.

Soft close first, so you hear the real objection. What time works tomorrow. On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you to start. If you're not a 10, what would get you there.

Book it live. Rookie calls a day apart. Call 3 at calendly.com/mathewjoyce/rookie-interview-call-3. Vets book the next call with me after proof is in.

Call 3, if they're in: If I offer you the seat, can you make trainings Wednesdays at 7 PM EST. Go over the 3-week commitment exactly the way the agreement has it. Promise nothing beyond that. No housing. No market. If they're in, offer the seat. The agreement goes out only after I OK it. Then set the 1-on-1 and send the training course.

If they say they need to think about it, this is my line: That's okay, I don't think this is gonna work out, we'll go with someone else. Then you wait. See if they fight for it. On a vet, you only use that line if I choose to with that person.

The draft scorecard says offer if they're 8 or higher on ready, and both coachability and work ethic are a clear yes. That's a draft, not a public claim. Don't say it as a company rule outside this room.

After a verbal yes, the agreement goes through Hawx Nidus, and only after I OK it. The 2027 rookie agreement still needs a legal review before it's treated as ready to send. Nothing gets signed without that.

Log the lead. Add rookies to GETHawx after call 2. Do not invent a signed status.

Vet close, short. Proof first, then fit, then a verbal yes, then the experienced or veteran 2027 agreement after I OK it. Travel and housing are after signing. They are never promised on the close.

Practice, about 5 minutes. Partner says I need to think about it. You use that takeaway line, then you wait. After a close, stop talking. Own the silence.

# 1:15-1:30  6. Objections and answers (15 min)

Facilitator notes, not spoken: these answers are training drafts, not approved outbound texts. They need Scoots's OK before this block is taught as his words. Do not use door objection rolls as recruit answers. Practice about 6 minutes. Stop and redo any answer that quotes pay, a bonus, housing, or a market.

Script:

Same formula we use at the door, used on recruit objections. No pay numbers in the answers.

One. Yes. Neutralize it. Yeah. Of course. Do not debate. Do not flinch.

Two. Address. Show you heard them. One relevant next step, not a speech.

Three. Close. Ask for the book, the names, or the decision. Then stop talking.

If your face argues, the words do not matter.

I need to think about it. You yes it, then you use my takeaway line, then you wait. If they fight for it, book the next call or move to the offer questions.

I have to talk to my parents. Yeah, that's expected. Tell them to talk before the next call. Book call 3 anyway. Do not bring parents a pay sheet in writing.

What's the pay, or they're shopping the deal. Pay is spoken on the call, scale on the screen, never in a text. Get them or keep them on the call. Do not quote a number in this room as a sample, and do not text one later.

Pushback on hours, or knocking until dark. Restate the schedule as it is. Meeting 9:30 to 10:30 AM. Knock noon to dark. Saturdays 10:30 AM to 5:30 PM. Sundays off. Season May 1 to August 31. If they will not run that, that's a Feet no.

Summer classes or a trip they will not move. That's a Feet red flag. Do not talk them into moving it. Mark no, or hold, and write the reason.

First questions are about time off. Same as hours. Get back to commitment. If that's the whole conversation, it's a no.

I can't think of any names. Stay on the ask. Prompt school, sports, work, last summer. Do not accept a stall as the end of the ask.

Vague, seems cool, no real why. Go back to Heart. If they still have no specific why, score it low and do not force a yes.

Vet number is vague, or they won't send proof. No proof, no call 2 as a vet. Hold, or move them to the rookie track. I decide.

They're under a contract, or talking to someone else at Hawx. Do not pitch. Contract, flag Counsel. One-house, I call that person first.

A vet wants terms in writing before committing. Deal terms stay spoken. Do not put numbers in a text to win the close.

Can I get housing, or which market. Do not promise either. If housing or a market exists for that person, it's whatever the agreement says, after paper.

Practice. I'm gonna throw three objections from that list. You answer Yes, Address, Close, then silence. If anyone quotes pay, a bonus, housing, or a market, we stop and redo it.

# 1:30-1:40  7. First-week playbook for a new rep (10 min)

Facilitator notes, not spoken: there is no standalone first-week playbook file. This is the week of the offer, months before May 1. Leave out dollars, bonuses, tiers, door-count targets, housing addresses, flights, and market assignments.

Script:

A new yes needs to know what happens in week one after the offer. You need to set it before you hang up.

This is the week of the offer, not the first week in season.

One. Paper. Agreement through Hawx Nidus, only after I OK the send. Legal review on the 2027 rookie agreement is still open, so don't talk like paper is already cleared to go. Background check when Hawx sends it. Read the 3-week commitment exactly as the agreement states. Do not add housing or market promises on top of it.

Two. GETHawx and the team group chat. Check phone and email. Respond to chat when you can.

Three. First 1:1, booked on the offer call, before they start the training course. They bring what they want out of the summer and what would stop them. Record the sunblock video, a short video to their future self on why they're doing this. Name a Board of Mentors, 2 to 3 people who keep them in the game.

Four. Training course and the pitch, sent after the agreement. Wednesday trainings, 7 PM EST, mandatory. Learn the road map: Intro, Bridge, Price Sheet, Objections, Close. Memorize the skeleton before they riff. Objection formula: Yes, Address, Close, then stop talking.

Five. Weekly 1:1 after that, 20 to 30 minutes. Last week's commitment, what's in the way, Mind, Heart, Feet, one thing the manager does for them, and question 10, three names. Book the next 1:1 before you hang up.

Schedule they should already have heard on call 2, so week one in season is not a surprise. Meeting 9:30 to 10:30 AM. Knock noon to dark. Saturdays 10:30 to 5:30. Sundays off. Season May 1 to August 31. Log sales in GETHawx the same day once they are in market.

Inputs only. No production targets. Pick a wake-up time and do not negotiate it. Morning routine. First door picked the night before. Stay out. Role play even if it's ten minutes. Reset every door. No negativity in the car group.

What we are not covering: dollars, bonuses, tiers, door-count targets, housing, flights, or market assignments. Those are not promises in this training.
